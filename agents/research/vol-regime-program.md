# Vol/Regime Experiment Program — Overview

**Author**: quant-researcher
**Date**: 2026-08-25
**Status**: PROGRAM PREREGISTERED, EXECUTION BLOCKED. Do not run any experiment in this program until the gating condition in §1 clears, except the pre-parity characterization carve-outs each experiment document names explicitly.

## 1. Gating condition — read this before running anything

**The measurement apparatus is not trustworthy today.** `GH #1106` (closed-candle-gating parity fix, branch `fix/closed-candle-gating`) is not merged. The forming-bar flip-rate study measured **43.2% of live decisions at minute 5 disagreeing with the closed-bar backtest decision**. Every experiment in this program compares sizing/exposure/exit-management overlays applied to HyperGrowth's existing, closed-bar-generated entries — which means:

- **Relative, single-worktree characterization runs** (Arm A vs Arm B vs Arm C, holding entries fixed) are **parity-insensitive** and are authorized to run now, labeled "pre-parity characterization" in every write-up. Each experiment document states this explicitly in its own §10.
- **Any promotion recommendation, staging trial, or absolute live-P&L claim is parity-sensitive** and is blocked until #1106 lands and a live-representative (or staging, flag-on) re-run reproduces the qualitative result. This is a hard gate, not a formality — the whole point of #1106 is that a closed-bar backtest may not be measuring what the live engine actually does, and sizing/exposure benefits measured against the wrong entry sequence could evaporate or reverse against the real one.

Nothing in this program authorizes a `src/` change, a staging deployment, or a live-config change on its own. Every experiment's "supported" branch terminates in "recommend to pm as a staging-trial candidate, contingent on #1106" — never a direct action.

## 2. What the evidence says, restated plainly

The 2026-08-13 model-free predictability scan (#1105) is the load-bearing input to this whole program. Its headline: **zero of 20 (symbol, timeframe) cells show a directional edge that clears the measured ~11bps round-trip cost.** Momentum accuracy sits below 0.50 in 11 of 20 cells; the best case (SOLUSDT/1d) is -2.64bps EV per trade, still negative. This closes, not narrows, the case for "a smarter model will find the edge" — six prior ETHUSDT/1h tournaments already converged on the same ~51-53% DA ceiling, and the scan shows that ceiling is not an ETHUSDT-specific or 1h-specific artifact; it is a property of the testable liquid-crypto space at this cost structure.

**A third, independent line of evidence converged on the same conclusion after this program's experiments were first drafted**, and is folded in here rather than treated as a footnote. GH #1067's Phase 1 conviction-sizing gate (`docs/research/experiments/2026-08-25_conviction-sizing-phase1.md`, PR #1116) tested whether the deployed ETHUSDT model's confidence/`|predicted_return|` magnitude predicts realized *direction*, on a fresh 7-week out-of-sample window against the actual production model. **It replicated GH #912's earlier null**: no directional signal (Cochran-Armitage p=0.967/0.996/0.967 across three bucket definitions). But its secondary statistic is decision-relevant here: `|predicted_return|` correlates with `|realized_return|` at **Spearman rho=0.204, p=1.5e-12** (n=1,177) — verified directly against the source document, not taken on relay. The model has no directional edge, but it appears to carry real (if modest — rho^2 is roughly 4% of variance) information about the *size* of the next move. Three independent studies — this one, the model-free scan's ARCH finding, and six prior tournaments' DA-ceiling convergence — now all point the same way: whatever exploitable structure exists in this data is second-moment (volatility), not first-moment (direction). This is treated as a **candidate input**, not an established fact — `2026-08-25_model-magnitude-vol-signal-prereg.md` (program item #0) is the preregistered test of whether it generalizes beyond the one model/window it was found on, before anything is built on top of it.

The scan itself found two more things that are direction-agnostic and strong:

| Statistic | Cells significant (of 20) | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| ARCH volatility clustering | **20/20** | Volatility today predicts volatility tomorrow, in every symbol/timeframe tested. |
| Regime persistence | **17/20** | Once in a high/low-vol regime, a bar tends to stay there (Markov P(stay) >> 0.5). |
| Directional baseline | 11/20 | Weak, inconsistent, and none clears cost. |
| Autocorrelation | 6/20 | Weak, inconsistent. |
| Variance ratio | 0/20 | No exploitable momentum/mean-reversion at any horizon tested. |
| Hurst | 0/20 | No persistent/trending structure beyond noise. |

Variance ratio and Hurst both landing at 0/20 is itself a decision-relevant negative finding: it directly rules out **vol-breakout and vol-compression-precedes-continuation entry strategies**, which depend on exactly the kind of price-path persistence these two statistics would have shown if it existed. That idea is not in this program's experiment list — its exclusion is evidence-driven, not an oversight (§4).

What ARCH and regime persistence *do* support is sizing and exposure control conditioned on volatility/regime state — a claim about defending against adverse excursions, not about predicting their direction or timing their start.

## 4. What this program deliberately does NOT test, and why

- **Vol-breakout / vol-compression directional entries** (e.g., "size up or enter after a period of compressed range, betting on the subsequent breakout continuing"). Excluded because the model-free scan's variance-ratio (0/20) and Hurst (0/20) statistics directly test and reject the premise this idea depends on — return persistence/trending behavior beyond a random walk. A vol-compression entry rule is a directional bet dressed in volatility language; the evidence that would need to exist for it to work (VR > 1 momentum, or H > 0.5 persistence) does not exist in any of the 20 tested cells. This is the evidence-driven exclusion the task explicitly asked for, not an oversight.
- **A non-crypto correlated reference asset for diversification.** The model-free scan already disclosed this gap (§2 of that document) — no equities/forex/commodities data provider is wired into `src/data_providers/`. Adding one is a data-infrastructure project, not a vol-regime experiment, and is out of scope for this program. Flagged for pm as a separate, larger backlog item if diversification-for-variance (program #4) graduates and the Board wants a genuinely uncorrelated leg rather than a second crypto symbol.
- **Any new ML model or feature.** This entire program is downstream of the finding that raw-price directional prediction is deprioritized across the testable space (#1105's pre-committed recommendation). Nothing here trains a model.
- **MFE-conditioned early-cut policy (GH #971).** The true version of "cut losers before they ride the full stop" requires a real `RiskParameters`/`ExitHandler` feature — `tp_06` (program #3) is the closest currently-expressible proxy, not a substitute. GH #971's build is money-path-adjacent `src/` work requiring `architecture-reviewer` + `risk-officer` review before any exam result from it counts, and is out of scope for a preregistration program that is explicitly config-only until #1106 lands. Named here so it isn't silently dropped from the Board's view of the returns-levers map.
- **Combined-mechanism arms** (e.g., vol-targeted sizing + regime-conditional exposure + `tp_06` all active simultaneously). Each individual document defers this explicitly (§12 of the exposure document, §11 of the sizing and diversification documents) to avoid an unattributable "kitchen sink" result. If two or more of #1-#3 graduate independently, a combined-mechanism preregistration is the natural next step — not run speculatively now.

## 5. Cross-cutting constraints applied to every experiment in this program

- **Drawdown-truncation discipline (#1102)**: every characterization run uses `--drawdown-cap-mode measure`; the `early_stopped` boolean is checked and reported for every single run, never assumed. Any run compared against another must use the same mode — a `measure`-mode run is never compared against an `enforce`-mode number. A confirmatory `enforce`-mode run is required before any recommendation reaches risk-officer. Not applicable to program item #0 (pure signal-scoring, no backtest engine involved).
- **Worktree import-path guard (#1070)**: every `atb` invocation across every experiment in this program is run as `PYTHONPATH="$(pwd)" atb ...` (or `PYTHONPATH="$(pwd)" python <script>` for #0's scoring script) from the worktree root, with the worktree's own code confirmed active before trusting any number — `2026-08-13_hypergrowth-tier-restore-reproduction.md` found a naive worktree invocation can silently execute the primary checkout's stale code with no error (+103% vs the intended window's honest -20%-ish number, purely from this defect). Every experiment document in this program states this explicitly in its own protocol section; it is restated here because it is easy to forget once a worktree "feels" set up correctly.
- **Explicit `--start`/`--end` calendar dates, never `--days N` relative to run-date**: the same #1070 investigation found a `--days 365`-relative-to-today run landed in an unrelated market regime purely from date arithmetic. Every backtest command in this program specifies calendar dates and records them exactly in its results appendix.
- **Cost realism**: `CostCalculator` defaults (fee_rate=0.001, slippage_rate=0.0005/side) stay on in every run, matching the ~11bps round-trip figure the model-free scan measured. No experiment in this program is ever reported "fee-free," even for a sanity check.
- **Provenance discipline (#1081)**: any document that cites a pre-2026-08-13-19:23 executed backtest number from the July research cluster either confirms that number's provenance first or, as the default and simpler path, re-derives its own control arm from scratch rather than trusting the cited figure. `2026-07-04_hypergrowth-365d-drawdown-stress-review.md` (explicitly flagged) and `2026-07-12_exit-geometry-honest.md` (unconfirmed either way) are the two documents this program's experiments touch; both are re-derived, not cited as data.
- **Sequential compute, no exceptions**: every experiment document commits to strictly sequential backtest runs, one at a time, in a single worktree, per the Mac-thermal hard rule. This applies across experiments too — do not run two of this program's five experiments' backtests (or #0's scoring script) concurrently in separate sessions/worktrees, even though nothing here is compute-heavy enough to be tempting; the rule is absolute, not a function of estimated load.
- **Determinism guard**: every experiment's control arm is run twice before the write-up is finalized; results must match to full float precision.
- **Fold/window discipline**: #1, #2, and #4 share an identical frozen exam window (2025-01-01 -> most recent complete UTC day) for direct cross-experiment comparability — deliberately different from GH #1071's `2025-07-04 -> 2026-07-04` window (see `2026-08-25_regime-conditional-exposure-prereg.md` §0.1/§5), since reusing #1071's exact window verbatim for #2 would not test the fold generalization that experiment is designed to provide. #3 uses the July study's F1/F2/F3 half-year folds plus three extended half-years (2021H2-2022H2), for reasons specific to that experiment's power problem (§4 of that document). #0 uses its own short scoring windows (§4 of that document, chosen to avoid overlap with the original 1,177-bar study). Every departure from the shared #1/#2/#4 window is deliberate and disclosed in its own document, not an inconsistency.
- **Trade-count floor**: ≥15 trades per arm/fold in every experiment, checked and reported, never assumed from historical trade counts.
- **Bonferroni, not BH-FDR, throughout this program.** Every experiment here is a confirmatory 2-4 arm comparison against a pre-committed bar, not a screen ranking many candidates (unlike #1105, which correctly used BH-FDR as a screening tool). This program inherits confirmatory statistics from `2026-07-12_exit-geometry-honest.md`'s and the tournament series' precedent, not the scan's screening precedent.

## 6. Honest assessment: does the evidence support a viable program here?

Yes, with real caveats stated up front rather than after the fact.

The case *for* this direction: ARCH clustering (20/20) and regime persistence (17/20) are not marginal, cherry-picked, or single-cell findings — they are close to universal across the tested grid, orders of magnitude more robust than anything the directional side of the same scan found. A third, independently-run study (GH #1067's Phase 1 gate, PR #1116) converged on the same conclusion from a completely different angle — a production model with a replicated-null directional signal nonetheless carries a real (p=1.5e-12, if modest at rho=0.204) volatility-magnitude signal. Three separate research efforts, three separate methodologies, one consistent answer: the exploitable structure in this data, to the extent any exists, is about how big moves are, not which way they go. The mechanisms this program tests (`VolatilityTargetSizer`, `RegimeAdaptiveSizer`, the graduated drawdown throttle, `ExposureGovernor`) mostly already exist in reviewed, parity-safe code and are either dormant, under-configured, or loosened relative to the Board-ratified defaults on the account trading live capital today — this is not a request to build a new subsystem, it is a request to rigorously test whether turning on or correctly configuring existing subsystems helps, which is a fundamentally cheaper and lower-risk research program than another model-training pass.

The case for caution: **none of these experiments can improve HyperGrowth's underlying expectancy.** The strategy's honest 365d backtest is -20.15%/21.84% MaxDD (pre-#1102-fix number, itself now suspect under the truncation defect and due for a `measure`-mode re-run as part of this program's own protocol) — a strategy with negative-to-flat expectancy that gets a smoother, lower-drawdown ride to the same negative-to-flat destination is a real risk-management improvement, not a profitability fix. The live account's own 18-trade sample (+$4.18 gross, 12W/6L, 2026-06-02 to 2026-08-20) is consistent with "barely trading, costs dominate at this size" — nothing in this program changes trade frequency or edge; program #4's own §12 shows the account is currently too small to even deploy its most capital-flexible idea (two-symbol diversification) without hitting minimum-notional constraints on a split allocation. If the Board's goal is return improvement, this program should be read as "make the existing (currently unprofitable-to-flat) strategy's risk profile more honest and better-controlled while the directional-edge question stays closed," not as a path back to profitability by itself. That is a legitimate, valuable, and clearly bounded goal — it should just not be oversold as more than that when results come back.

This program does not, by itself, resolve the account's core problem — it is barely trading at a size where costs dominate, per the live 18-trade sample (+$4.18 gross, 12W/6L, 2026-06-02 to 2026-08-20), and nothing here changes trade frequency or produces a return edge. Whether that means "wait for #1106 and this program's risk-quality improvements, and accept HyperGrowth's expectancy is what it is" or "the honest answer is this strategy/symbol needs to be reconsidered entirely" is a pm/Board-level question this program does not answer and is not designed to. It answers a narrower, well-evidenced question: given that direction is closed, does the volatility/regime structure that clearly exists get us anything. That is worth doing. It should not be mistaken for more than that.
